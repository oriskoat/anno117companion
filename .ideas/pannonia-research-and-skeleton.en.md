# Pannonia: historical research and implementation framework

*Earlier research foundation and design skeleton.* [Read the concept](README.md) · [Current population and needs design, version 2](pannonia-population-needs.en.md)

This document preserves the research framework and early alternatives behind the concept. Its preliminary tier names, need lists and Marvel assignments were subsequently refined in the version 2 plan. Use that plan for the current proposed roster; use this document for geography, historical framing, reuse opportunities and implementation questions.

## 1. Geography and chronology

The territory of present-day Hungary was not identical to the Roman province of Pannonia in AD 117. Transdanubia belonged to Pannonia Superior and Pannonia Inferior. Much of the plain east of the Danube lay outside Roman provincial territory, in the Barbaricum associated with the Iazygian Sarmatians. The setting should not be presented as a uniform “Hungarian,” ethnically Pannonian or Celtic region.

The playable core is Transdanubia and the Danube frontier. The eastern plain could support a distinct diplomatic, commercial or Sarmatian frontier system rather than being absorbed into the same provincial population.

## 2. Landscape: what Albion can contribute

Albion's wetland system is a technical donor, not a cultural template. Reuse opportunities include:

- Marshland as a separate building surface.
- Fishing/reed buildings restricted to wet ground.
- A limited wetland building selection and dedicated road connections.
- Wooden walkways, embankments and raised access integrated with ordinary roads.
- Shallow-water, backwater and floodplain fishing.
- Reeds as construction or craft inputs.

The region should not become one enormous swamp. Wetland districts can draw on Danube floodplains/islands, the Rába–Rábca–Hanság landscape, the Sárvíz valley, the Zala mouth and western/southwestern Pelso margins. Dry alluvial ridges, loess, hills, forests, pasture and cultivated land are equally important.

## 4. Population architecture

### Initial branching proposal

```text
T1 — Frontier Communities
 ├─ T2 — Civitas Provincials
 │    └─ T3 — Pannonian Notables
 └─ T2 — Citizens
      └─ T3 — Decurions
```

Version 2 refines these names to **Provinciales → Vicani → Principes** and **Provinciales → Municipes → Decuriones**.

- **Frontier Communities:** an inclusive starting population of local civitas members, rural settlers, craftspeople, traders, military families and immigrants.
- **Civitas Provincials:** a path built on villages/vici, local institutions, livestock, crafts and syncretic cults, rather than a “primitive” or “tribal” branch.
- **Pannonian Notables:** landowners, patrons and civitas leaders; a regional elite, not a uniform ethnicity.
- **Citizens:** urban/legal, military and commercial integration. Citizenship was a meaningful distinction in AD 117; the general imperial grant followed in AD 212.
- **Decurions:** the local council elite of coloniae and municipia.

The branches describe institutions and consumption, not two sealed populations. Albion's branching mechanic can support regional names, art, cults and economic emphasis.

### Conservative alternative

A single T1 → T2 → T3 population ladder with local-civitas or Roman-urban neighbourhood specialisations would emphasise interconnection, but reuse less of Albion's existing branching model.

**Initial direction:** retain two paths, with an institutional rather than ethnic distinction and room for movement between them.

### Strong historical production foundation

Grain, husbandry, fishing, forestry/timber, wool, leather, textiles, everyday clothing, pottery, bricks/tiles, quarrying, ironworking, smithing, bronze crafts, military provisions, storage, transport, local markets and river/road trade form the starting palette.

### Subjects requiring care

- **Wine:** allow it without projecting the later intensive villa/vineyard economy around Pelso fully back to AD 117.
- **Mining:** stone, clay and smaller ironworking are stronger foundations than a local gold/silver mining economy. Dacian gold is not a Pannonian local resource.
- **Salt:** primarily an import and trade input.
- **Amber:** an Amber Road commodity, not a locally extracted material.

## 7. Settlements and regional roles

- **Aquincum:** legionary fortress, canabae, governor's centre and a developing civilian settlement in AD 117. River logistics, waterworks and baths. Avoid importing its later municipal status or fully developed urban form into the starting date.
- **Brigetio:** Trajanic legionary fortress, military supply/manufacturing and a Danube–road junction.
- **Savaria:** Claudian colonia and veteran settlement on the Amber Road; a strong early Roman urban anchor.
- **Scarbantia:** Flavian municipium, western transport/amber gateway and forum/Capitolium.
- **Gorsium:** emerging civilian/sacred settlement after the camp's closure around AD 105–106. Its identification as the principal provincial assembly/imperial-cult centre is contested; label the interpretation as reconstruction.
- **Pelso / Lake Balaton:** fishing, livestock, early farms/estates, smaller vineyards and waterside management. The later luxury-villa landscape should not be the AD 117 starting condition.

## 8. Ripa Pannonica — the Danube frontier project

Rather than a single invented monument, the region's overarching undertaking would extend across several sites:

1. Limes road and milestones.
2. Watchtowers and signal stations.
3. Auxiliary forts.
4. River harbours and crossings.
5. Granaries and supply stations.
6. Brigetio and Aquincum's legionary centres as anchors.

Possible benefits include road speed, storage, Danube trade, military provisioning, frontier security and regional prestige. This expresses the region's logistical identity through a connected system.

## 9. Chronological guardrails for AD 117

- Do not start with Aquincum as a complete later municipal metropolis.
- Its later stone amphitheatres should not be starting buildings.
- Do not present Gorsium's monumental/provincial cult role as uncontested.
- Savaria's late Roman palace does not belong in the starting setting.
- A monumental Iseum requires careful treatment as a possible later development.
- The late Roman villas around Balaton and the world associated with the Seuso treasure are not an AD 117 baseline.
- Galerius' Pelso water-management works belong to the third/fourth-century context, not a historical project already available in AD 117.

## 10. Implementation and design sequence

The early research suggested the following workflow:

1. Choose branching population or neighbourhood specialisation.
2. Review tier names linguistically and against inscriptional evidence.
3. Audit base-game assets/GUIDs using the complete need grid and construction menu.
4. Identify goods that can be reused directly.
5. Prioritise a small first scope of two to four genuinely new regional goods/services.
6. Assign normal T3 Marvels with art costs and mechanics in mind.
7. Consolidate the design into a readable public concept.

The concept and version 2 plan now document the proposed direction. Asset/GUID auditing, feasibility work and balancing remain implementation tasks. The wider list of possible new systems in version 2 should not be mistaken for a commitment to build them all in the first playable scope.
