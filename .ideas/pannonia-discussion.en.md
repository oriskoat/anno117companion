# What if Pannonia became Anno 117's Danube frontier, orchard and wetland region?

I have been thinking about how **Pannonia**, focused specifically on the territory of modern Hungary in AD 117, could work as an Anno 117 region.

The obvious starting point is Albion — but only technically, not culturally.

Large parts of the relevant landscape were shaped by the Danube and its islands, floodplains, oxbows and reed beds, alongside places such as the Hanság, the Sárvíz valley and the western wetlands around Lake Pelso, today's Lake Balaton. Albion already gives the game marsh building surfaces, restricted wetland placement, eels, reeds and wooden access routes. Those systems could be reused without pretending that Pannonia was somehow “Albion with Roman forts.”

At the same time, this should not turn the whole region into one giant swamp. Pannonia also needs dry ridges, cereal fields, orchards, vineyards, oak forests, pasture, clay pits, roads, villas, civilian settlements and the heavily militarised Danube frontier.

My general rule would be:

> Reuse Albion for wetland terrain and population branching, reuse Latium for Roman urban life, and spend new art and production work only where it creates a recognisably Pannonian economy.

## One shared tier, followed by two development paths

I would use Albion's branching structure, but the branches would represent institutions and civic status rather than two isolated ethnic populations.

```text
T1 – Provinciales
 ├─ T2 – Vicani
 │    └─ T3 – Principes
 └─ T2 – Municipes
      └─ T3 – Decuriones
```

- **Provinciales:** the shared provincial population — local civitas members, settlers, craftspeople, traders and military families.
- **Vicani:** residents following the village, vicus and local civitas path.
- **Principes:** the leading families and notables of the local civitates.
- **Municipes:** residents developing through Roman municipal institutions and citizenship.
- **Decuriones:** the council elite of municipia and coloniae.

The Vicani path is not a “primitive native” branch, and the Municipes are not necessarily immigrants from Italy. They are two ways in which a mixed frontier society can develop.

## The need structure

The proposal follows Anno 117's existing cumulative content grid:

| Tier | New Food | New Public Service | New Fashion | New Household | New Marvel |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| T1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | – | – |
| T2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | – |
| T3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

These are available cumulative need slots, not twenty-four individually mandatory boxes. Anno 117's supply-point thresholds would still allow different combinations and optional over-fulfilment.

## T1 – Provinciales

### Food

- **Grain Porridge**
- **Danube Fish**

Danube Fish would use two possible production methods for the same good: an open-water Fishing Hut based on Latium, or a backwater Eel Grabber based on Albion. This would let dry shores and marsh islands solve the same need through different layouts.

### Public services

- **Market**
- **Wayside Shrine — Sacellum**

The shrine could later be dedicated to a selected local or Romanised cult, using the mechanical idea behind Albion's Fanum without importing Albion's gods.

### Fashion

- **Tunics**
- **Wool Cloaks**

This makes wool one of the region's basic materials from the start and prepares later clothing, military and horse-equipment chains.

## The local-provincial path

## T2 – Vicani

### Food

- **Pannonian Grain Beer**
- **Fruit Baskets**

The beer could reuse Albion's brewing chain with barley or millet. The later recorded name *sabaia/sabaea* could appear in flavour text, although I would keep the safer UI name “Pannonian Grain Beer” for an AD 117 setting.

Fruit Baskets could become one of Pannonia's signature production chains:

```text
Orchard Fruit + Woven Basket
→ Fruit Basket
```

The important part is that **Woven Baskets would also be an independent Household need**. Households and fruit merchants would therefore compete for the same supply.

```text
2 Orchard Fruit + 1 Woven Basket
→ 1 Fruit Basket
```

The basket would be consumed as an abstraction of wear, damage and continuous replacement, so the game would not need an empty-container return system.

### Public services

- **Vicus Hall**
- **Civitas Sanctuary**

The Vicus Hall could increase nearby craft productivity or workforce. The sanctuary could offer a small selection of historically checked Pannonian-Roman cults, with bonuses for livestock, woodland, river production, crafts or military supply.

### Fashion

- **Wool Trousers — Braccae**
- **Norico-Pannonian Fibulae**

The trousers could reuse Albion's existing clothing logic, but with Pannonian art and context. Fibulae are a much stronger archaeological marker than inventing a completely fictional “tribal costume,” and they should not be presented as belonging to only one ethnicity.

### Household

- **Pannonian Pottery**
- **Woven Baskets**

```text
Reeds or Willow Rods
→ Basket Weaver
→ Woven Baskets
```

This is where Albion's reeds become truly Pannonian. The output is not a renamed reed shoe: it supports households, fruit growing, fishing, harvest logistics, storage and eventually the army.

## T3 – Principes

### Food

- **Smoked Meats**
- **Pelso Wine**

Pelso Wine would represent a late-game vineyard economy created by the player around Lake Balaton. It should not claim that the later Roman luxury-villa landscape was already fully developed in AD 117. Imported wine could remain an alternative.

And no, it cannot be Tokaji — Tokaj was east of the Roman frontier, and “Tokaji” belongs to a vastly later historical world. :D

### Public services

- **Civitas Council House**
- **Sacred Spring Baths**

The Council House could provide decree slots for agriculture, crafts, trade or limes supply. The spring baths would combine a local sacred-water site with Roman bathing practice rather than forcing a monumental aqueduct into every rural settlement.

### Fashion

- **Fine Wool Mantles**
- **Amber Jewellery**

Amber would be imported along the Amber Road and worked locally around the Savaria–Scarbantia trade axis. It would not be mined in Pannonia.

### Household

- **Decorated Pottery**
- **Riding Horses**

Decorated Pottery could develop from the same clay economy as the earlier Pannonian Pottery, eventually producing Pannonian slipped tableware. Riding Horses would be the civilian and prestige side of a much larger horse economy.

### Marvels

- **Gorsium Sacred Precinct**
- **Aquincum Spring and Bath District**

Gorsium works especially well as something the player develops after the military site was dismantled around AD 105–106. I would avoid presenting it as the undisputed official headquarters of the provincial imperial cult, because that interpretation is debated.

The Aquincum bath project would be built around spring capture, an aqueduct, baths and a sanctuary precinct. Aquincum's aqueduct was supplied by springs in the area now called Rómaifürdő, so Hungary's modern thermal-water identity has a genuine Roman-era foundation here.

## The municipal-Roman path

## T2 – Municipes

### Food

- **Bread**
- **Garum**

Both already exist in the game. Garum would require trade or salt supply rather than pretending that Pannonia had a coastline.

### Public services

- **Public Baths**
- **Mansio — Road Station**

The Mansio could boost nearby cart speed, roads and trade deliveries, giving Pannonia's military roads and the Amber Road a real gameplay function.

### Fashion

- **Sandals**
- **Fine Tunics**

### Household

- **Amphorae**
- **Terracotta Oil Lamps**

Aquincum's military pottery and lamp production would give the lamp chain a clear regional context while still reusing Latium's clay economy.

## T3 – Decuriones

### Food

- **Imported Vintage Wine**
- **Dried and Honeyed Fruits**

The second good would turn the local orchard surplus into an elite product. Imported figs or dates could act as alternative inputs, but they should not grow locally.

### Public services

- **Forum and Basilica**
- **Grammaticus School**

### Fashion

- **Togas**
- **Silver Fibulae**

### Household

- **Writing Tablets**
- **Fine Glassware**

### Marvels

- **Aquincum Governor's Palace**
- **Savaria Forum and Capitolium**

Aquincum should begin as a legionary, administrative and developing civilian centre, not as the complete later second- or third-century metropolis. Savaria is the safer early monumental anchor: it was already an established Claudian veteran colony on the Amber Road.

## Orchards should be a real regional system

Modern Hungary's fruit-growing identity also has useful ancient foundations, but I would avoid creating six nearly identical crop chains.

A modular Orchard could offer different visual plantings:

```text
Orchard
 ├─ Apple and Pear Grove
 ├─ Plum and Cherry Grove
 └─ Walnut and Peach Grove — later upgrade
        ↓
     Orchard Fruit
```

All variants would produce the same manageable input, perhaps with small differences in yield, seasonality or fertility. Apples, pears, plums, cherries, walnuts, peaches and grapes all belong in the broader Roman Pannonian discussion, although the exact AD 117 chronology needs care. Figs, dates and olives would remain imported goods.

Wild elderberries, sloes, cornelian cherries and hazelnuts could also support a small forest-edge gathering mechanic without each becoming a separate population need.

## Horses, cavalry and bows

Horses should be another major Pannonian system, not merely a reference to much later Hungarian history.

```text
Horse Pasture
→ Horse Breeder
→ Training Paddock
 ├─ Riding Horses
 ├─ Draught Horses
 └─ Military Horses + Tack
```

Riding Horses fulfil the Principes' Household need. Draught Horses improve carts and agricultural logistics. Military Horses, together with leather and metal tack, equip auxiliary cavalry at forts.

Bows need a historical distinction:

- locally produced hunting bows and Roman auxiliary archery are reasonable military chains;
- **Iazygian horse archers belong to the Sarmatian Iazyges east of the Danube**, not to Pannonia and certainly not to some fictional “ancient Hungarian” population.

The Iazyges were Roman allies under Trajan, so a Danube trade or alliance treaty could unlock expensive allied horse archers requiring both Riding Horses and Reflex Bows. This would turn the eastern bank into a diplomatic frontier rather than empty map scenery.

## The special project above the normal Marvels: Ripa Pannonica

Instead of inventing one enormous fantasy temple, Pannonia's special third project could be a distributed **Danube frontier system**:

```text
Limes Road and Milestones
→ Watchtowers and Signal Stations
→ Auxiliary Forts
→ River Harbours and Crossings
→ Granaries and Supply Stations
→ Brigetio Legionary Fortress
→ Aquincum Command and Logistics Centre
```

The two population paths would contribute different supplies.

**Principes:** grain, meat, wool cloaks, baskets, timber, draught horses and riding horses.

**Decuriones:** bricks, ironwork, amphorae, writing tablets, money, engineers and administrative capacity.

Completing the Ripa Pannonica could improve road and river transport, warehouse capacity, military security, imperial demand for equipment and diplomatic trade across the Danube.

This would make the final project the economic integration of the whole region, not just another large building.

## How the major locations would fit

- **Aquincum:** government, legionary logistics, springs, aqueducts, baths and the developing civilian centre.
- **Brigetio:** legionary fortress, military manufacturing and the main anchor of the Ripa Pannonica.
- **Savaria:** veteran colony, Roman urbanism and Amber Road commerce.
- **Scarbantia:** western road and amber-trade gateway.
- **Gorsium:** a former military site developing into a new civilian and sacred centre.
- **Pelso:** orchards, vineyards, fishing, livestock and developing villa farms rather than an already completed late-Roman luxury landscape.

One important geographical caveat: in AD 117, Roman Pannonia covered western Hungary, while most of the Great Hungarian Plain east of the Danube belonged to the Iazygian Sarmatian world. That division is not a problem for the concept — it gives the region a meaningful frontier and diplomacy system.

## Why I think this could work

Pannonia would not need dozens of completely new goods.

It could reuse:

- Albion's marsh surfaces, eels, reeds, restricted wetland building and branching population structure;
- Latium's bread, garum, wine, baths, amphorae, oil lamps, forum, school, toga and glassware;
- existing livestock, horse, clay, textile, metalworking, road, warehouse and military systems.

The new-content budget could then focus on a few things that genuinely matter:

- Woven Baskets and Fruit Baskets;
- modular Orchards;
- Pannonian Pottery and Amber Jewellery;
- the Vicus Hall and Civitas Sanctuary;
- horse breeding, cavalry and Iazygian diplomacy;
- the Aquincum Spring and Bath District;
- the Ripa Pannonica.

That feels more convincing to me than either “Albion with different names” or a region containing forty new production chains simply for novelty.
